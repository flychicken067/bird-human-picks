How we choose

How each photo was chosen, what it shows, and what it does not.

1,209 species so far. Data collection is still running, so more will be added.

Steps

  1. The checklist follows AviList 2026; only species whose boundaries did not change are included.
  2. A wild, research-grade observation on iNaturalist that at least 3 people currently identify as this species, in at least 3 different months, with nobody naming another species.
  3. Only photos licensed CC0, CC BY or CC BY-SA, at least 1200 px on the long side.
  4. A detection model only checks how many birds are in the frame, how big, and whether a person or hand is visible. It never decides the species.
  5. Every photo was looked at. Hidden, partial, dead, domestic, drawn or wrong birds were removed: 367 photos, each with a recorded reason.
  6. Every photo has a report link, and corrections are made in public.

Questions

Why photos and not AI drawings?

We want people to decide whether the species is right. The photos are taken by people and identified by several people, so every step traces back to someone. The cost: mixed styles, and fewer species than drawing them all.

How accurate is it?

More reliable than a single identification, but 3 accounts are not 3 experts, and they may follow each other. No ornithologist checked every photo; use a proper field guide for fine details.

I found a mistake.

Every species page has a report link with the bird's name filled in. Say what is wrong and how you know; we will replace the photo and note it in the changelog.

I'm a photographer and don't want my photo here.

Open an issue on GitHub with the bird's name or the photo link. We will replace it within 7 days and not use it again.

How do I credit a photo?

Credit the photographer under the photo's own license and mention iNaturalist. Each species page has a ready-made credit line. Changed CC BY-SA photos must be shared under the same license.

How does this relate to Huashu's stickers?

Huashu's huashu-bird-sticker gives every bird an AI-generated sticker. We wanted to see how far human judgement alone can go, so each species can be compared with his sticker. Which has fewer mistakes will be judged by the errors people report once both are public.

Sources

  • Taxonomy, size and conservation status: data compiled by huashu-bird-sticker (MIT); size from AVONET and field guides, status from AviList (BirdLife).
